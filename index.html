About Onam

Discover the story behind Kerala's most celebrated festival.

The Festival of Joy

Onam is one of the most important festivals celebrated in Kerala. It brings families and communities together with colorful celebrations, traditional games, music, dance and delicious food.

The festival is traditionally associated with the return of King Mahabali, whose reign is remembered as a golden age of prosperity and happiness.

10 Days of Onam

The traditional journey from Atham to Thiruvonam

Day 1

Atham

The first day of Onam celebrations begins with the creation of the Pookalam.

Day 2

Chithira

Families add more flowers and patterns to their beautiful Pookalam.

Day 3

Chodhi

Preparations for the grand celebration continue across Kerala.

Day 4

Vishakam

Markets become busy as families prepare traditional dishes and celebrations.

Day 5

Anizham

The excitement grows as Vallam Kali boat race preparations take place.

Day 6

Thrikketa

Families begin preparing for the main celebrations and gatherings.

Day 7

Moolam

Traditional dances, feasts and cultural events become more prominent.

Day 8

Pooradam

Onathappan idols are placed in the Pookalam as preparations continue.

Day 9

Uthradam

Known as the day of great preparations before the main celebration.

Day 10

Thiruvonam

The grandest day of Onam, celebrated with Sadya, Pookalam and family gatherings.

🍛 Onam Sadya

A delicious feast that brings Kerala's flavors together.

Avial

A traditional Kerala dish made with mixed vegetables, coconut and yogurt.

Sambar

A flavorful lentil and vegetable curry enjoyed as an essential part of Sadya.

Thoran

A dry vegetable preparation cooked with coconut and aromatic spices.

Olan

A mild traditional dish prepared with ash gourd, beans and coconut milk.

Pachadi

A refreshing side dish combining vegetables, yogurt and coconut.

Payasam

A sweet traditional dessert that completes the grand Onam feast.
